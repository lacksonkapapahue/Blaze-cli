# Email and message drafting

**Never send on the user's behalf without showing the full draft (recipients, subject, body)
and getting an explicit yes** - unless the user gave a standing rule for that kind of message.
Draft mode (gmail_draft) is fine without asking.

## 1. Get the context

- Who is it to, what's the relationship (client, boss, friend, stranger), and what has been said before (read the thread; `recall` memory for the person and the user's tone).
- The **one outcome** wanted: a reply, a meeting, a payment, a yes/no, goodwill.
- Facts that must be included: dates, amounts, links, attachments. Don't invent any; use `[placeholder]`.
- Channel: email (subject + body), chat (shorter, no sign-off), proposal platform (rules of that site).

## 2. Write it

- **Subject**: specific and scannable ("Invoice #214 - due 15 Oct", "Quick question about the Shopify theme"); for replies keep the thread subject.
- **First line = the point** (the ask or the answer). Context after, only as much as needed.
- Short paragraphs (1-3 sentences), ≤ 150 words for most emails; bullets for several items or options.
- Make the ask explicit and easy: a yes/no question, two time options, a deadline with a reason.
- Match the user's voice and the recipient's formality; mirror their language. Warm, never grovelling; firm, never rude.
- Close with the next step, then the user's usual sign-off.

## 3. Patterns

- **Follow-up**: "Following up on my note from Tuesday about X - could you confirm Y by Friday?" One line of value, one ask.
- **Saying no**: thank → clear no → brief reason (optional) → alternative if any.
- **Apology**: own it in one sentence → what happened (no excuses) → what's done to fix it → what changes.
- **Complaint/request to a company**: order/account number first, what happened with dates, what you want (refund/replacement/by when), attachments.
- **Cold outreach**: why them (specific) → what you offer in one line with proof → small ask (15-min call) → easy out.
- **Freelance proposal (Upwork/Fiverr)**: open with their problem in your words, 1-2 relevant results with numbers or links, a mini plan (3 steps), timeline and price if asked, one question that shows you read the brief. 120-200 words. No "Dear Sir/Madam", no copy-paste feel.
- **Client update**: status in one line (on track / at risk) → done → next → blockers / decisions needed from them.

## 4. Before showing the draft

- [ ] Recipient address verified from a previous email, not guessed.
- [ ] Names spelled right; dates with weekday; amounts with currency; links work; attachments mentioned are actually attached.
- [ ] Tone check: would the user say this out loud to this person?
- [ ] Shown as: To / Cc / Subject / Body, then ask "Send it, or change anything?"
- [ ] After sending: confirm in one line; offer a reminder to follow up if no reply.
